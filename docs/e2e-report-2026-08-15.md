## Why the earlier chat missed Spark

The older 33-tool, 148-second turn did not contain an `ask_bot` or `send_bot` activity. Its reasoning said it would hold off on routing. The failure had four causes:

1. The Chief of Staff prompt said “Route work” but did not define a role map or require a handoff for specialist-owned work.
2. `list_bots` exposed only name, model, and busy state. It did not expose the role title, description, or built-in skills needed to choose Spark.
3. Onboarding said the library included six specialist roles, while a fresh workspace only created Chief of Staff and Research. Spark was available in the library, but not yet in the roster.
4. The watchdog could stop a coordinator while it waited for a slow peer. The no-event threshold was shorter than a real research handoff.

## Changes made

- Added deterministic, high-confidence routing hints for the Chief of Staff. The hints match writing, research, build, communications, operations, and creative work to visible teammate metadata.
- Added multi-role routing. A task that contains research and writing can be handed to Research first and Spark second.
- Added role title, description, and built-in skill slugs to the internal agent roster and `list_bots` output.
- Strengthened the Chief of Staff routing policy. It now requires `list_bots` and `ask_bot` before specialist work, and it uses configured role metadata as the source of truth. A custom Spark role that says “writing and research” is supported.
- Added a watchdog heartbeat while `ask_bot` waits for a peer. This prevents a long peer turn from being marked as stuck while the caller is still making progress.
- Corrected onboarding copy. It now says the six specialist roles are offered in the library and tells the user to add roles from the `+` button.
- Added routing unit coverage and extended the agent-proxy roster test.
